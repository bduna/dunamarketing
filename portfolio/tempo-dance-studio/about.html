About

Who runs it

Eleven years dancing, three in a church basement

I danced professionally for eleven years and taught out of a church basement for three before Tempo opened in 2018.

Two things I want this place known for: technique that holds up, and a studio where nobody body-shames a kid.

Kids here get corrected on their turnout, their timing and their line. Never on their bodies.

Adults get the same room and the same barre. Drop in for $22, stand at the back if you'd rather, and start whenever you're ready to start.

The church basement taught me what actually matters in a studio: not the mirrors, the teaching. Tempo has better floors and better light, but the classes run the way they always have — small enough that every dancer gets corrected by name, every single class.

Fall to spring

What a year here looks like

Classes build from September toward the recital — a real stage, a real audience, and one $95 fee that covers the whole production. Nobody is required to perform; plenty of adults take class all year and never set foot on stage, and that is a fine way to use this studio.

The competition team runs alongside the regular schedule. It’s entered by audition rather than sign-up, and if you ask whether you’re ready, you’ll get a straight answer — that’s cheaper than finding out at the audition.

Everything in between is the unglamorous part: turnout, timing, line, drilled until they hold under pressure. That’s what “technique that holds up” means here — not a slogan, the actual lesson plan.
