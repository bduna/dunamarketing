Who you are handing it to

Same presses, same neighborhood.

My parents bought this shop in 1994 and I took it over in 2015. Same presses, same neighborhood, same Saturday regulars — a few of whom have been bringing in shirts for longer than I have been running the place.

What changed between those two dates is mostly nothing, and that is on purpose. The equipment that did the job then does it now. The price list is still short enough to read in one go, and there is nothing on it that turns into a different number at the till.

“We actually get the stain out, and we tell you honestly when we can’t.”

That is what we are known for, and the second half is the half that matters. Nobody enjoys being told a mark is not coming out. It still beats paying to be disappointed twice.

— Maple Street Cleaners, Cincinnati, Ohio

At the counter

Thirty seconds that decide the rest.

Most of what we need to know, you already know. Say these three things when you hand the piece over and you will get a straight answer on the spot instead of a phone call later.

  1. 1

    Point out the mark

    Show us where it is rather than leaving us to find it. It takes ten seconds and it changes what gets tried first.

  2. 2

    Say what caused it, if you know

    Wine, oil, ink and “no idea” are four different problems. Even a guess from you is worth more than nothing.

  3. 3

    Say when you need it back

    The date decides everything else. In before ten and same-day is usually possible; if it is tight you get a yes or a no there and then.

What it costs

No asterisks, and no sixth line at the till.

Five things, five numbers. Where a line says “from”, the garment decides the rest — never the day of the week and never how busy we are. Everything is priced per piece, so a bag of five is five prices you can add up yourself before you hand it over.

Not sure which line your jacket falls on? Call and describe it. We will price it before you make the trip.
