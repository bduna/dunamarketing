About Lumen

Twelve years of academic surgery, then this.

I trained at Johns Hopkins and spent twelve years in academic surgery before opening Lumen. I take on fewer cases so I can do them properly.

What I’m known for is results that look like you, not like surgery — and never pushing a patient toward more than they came for. Most people who sit down here have been researching quietly for months and want one thing from a surgeon: the truth about what’s realistic.

Academic surgery is a good teacher and a demanding one. You operate with people watching, you publish what went wrong as well as what went right, and you learn that the best outcome is the one nobody notices. I opened Lumen because I wanted the other half of the job back — the long conversation before anything gets scheduled.

Half of that conversation is about what not to do. If the honest answer is that you don’t need an operation, you will hear it here — people have been talked out of procedures in this office more often than they’ve been talked into them.

A word before you book

Who this practice is for.

Not every practice suits every patient, and it is better to say so on a website than in a consultation room. A rough guide:

Probably you, if…

  • You’ve been reading about this for a long time and want a plain answer about what’s realistic before you commit to anything.
  • You’d rather look rested than look different, and you’d like the people around you not to be able to say why.
  • You want a surgeon who is willing to say no.

Probably not, if…

  • You want the most dramatic change that can be achieved, rather than the smallest one that solves the problem.
  • You’d rather be told what to have done than talk it through.
  • You’re deciding on price alone. That is a fair way to shop; it just isn’t what this practice is built around.
