About Lumen

Twelve years of academic surgery, then this.

I trained at Johns Hopkins and spent twelve years in academic surgery before opening Lumen. I take on fewer cases so I can do them properly.

In my own words

What I am known for is results that look like you, not like surgery — and never pushing a patient toward more than they came for. Most people who sit down here have been researching quietly for months and want one thing from a surgeon: the truth about what is realistic.

Academic surgery is a good teacher and a demanding one. You operate with people watching. You publish what went wrong as well as what went right. And you learn, quickly, that the best outcome is the one nobody notices.

I opened Lumen because I wanted the other half of the job back — the long conversation before anything gets scheduled. That conversation is the reason the list of procedures is short, and the reason the diary is not full.

Half of it is about what not to do. If the honest answer is that you do not need an operation, you will hear it in this office — people have been talked out of procedures here more often than they have been talked into them.
