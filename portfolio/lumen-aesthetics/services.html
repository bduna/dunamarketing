Before you book

Questions people ask first.

Do I need to know what I want before the consultation?

No. Most people arrive with a feeling rather than a procedure name — something about the nose, a tiredness around the eyes that sleep does not fix. Describing it in your own words is the right place to start. Putting a name to it, or deciding it does not need one, is my job.

What does the $200 actually cover?

Time, mostly. We go through what is bothering you, what surgery could realistically change, what it could not, and whether a non-surgical option — or no treatment at all — makes more sense. If you go ahead with surgery, the $200 is credited toward it.

Why is surgery not priced on the website?

Because every face and every case is different. A number posted before we have met would be either wrong or padded to be safe. You will get a specific quote after your consultation, for the operation we have actually agreed on.

Will you tell me if I should not have surgery?

Yes, and it happens more often than you would think. Never pushing a patient toward more than they came in for is the closest thing this practice has to a rule. If the honest answer is to do less, or to do nothing, that is what you will hear.

Are injectables a way to avoid surgery?

Sometimes, and sometimes they are a detour. It depends on what is actually behind what you are seeing in the mirror, which is a question for the consultation rather than for a website.
