The price comes first

Why you hear the number before I drive.

A locksmith who turns up and then names a price has you over a barrel. You are locked out, it is late, and what are you going to do — call someone else and wait another hour? I don’t work that way. You hear the price on the phone, you say yes or no, and if it is yes, the number doesn’t change when I get there.

The other half of the job is quieter than lockouts: rekeys and deadbolts for people who just bought a place, or just want to stop wondering who else has a key. Same rule — priced per lock, on the phone, before I come out.

Straight answers

Rekey or replace? Usually rekey.

People assume a lost key means new locks. Most of the time it doesn’t. Rekeying keeps the lock that is already on your door and changes the pins inside it, so a new key works and every old one stops working. It is quicker, it is from $25 a lock, and the hardware you paid for stays where it is.

Replacing makes sense when the lock itself is the problem — a deadbolt that has given up, a door that never had one, or builder-grade hardware you would rather upgrade. A new deadbolt fitted and keyed to work starts at $120. Describe the door on the phone and I’ll tell you which one you actually need before I drive.

  • Rekey when you’ve moved in, lost a key, or someone who used to have one shouldn’t any more.
  • Replace when the lock is worn or broken, the door has no deadbolt, or you want something stronger than what the builder fitted.
